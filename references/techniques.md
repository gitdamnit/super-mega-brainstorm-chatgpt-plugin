## A. Problem Framing Techniques

### A1. How Might We (HMW) Ladder
**Purpose:** Refine a problem statement by climbing up and down the abstraction ladder.

**When to use:** Problem statement keeps wobbling or feels too vague/constrained.

**How to apply:**
1. Start with the current problem statement
2. Ask "How might we..." and generate 3 more abstract versions
3. Ask "How might we..." and generate 3 more specific versions
4. Pick the framing that best balances specificity and flexibility

**Example:**
- Original: "How do we get more newsletter subscribers?"
- More abstract: "How might we grow our audience?", "How might we increase engagement?", "How might we create value for more people?"
- More specific: "How might we get coffee enthusiasts to subscribe?", "How might we convert existing readers' friends?", "How might we leverage our archive content?"

---

### A2. The 5 Whys
**Purpose:** Find the root cause of a problem by repeatedly asking "why".

**When to use:** Problem seems symptomatic rather than fundamental.

**How to apply:**
1. State the problem
2. Ask "Why does this happen?" and answer
3. Take that answer and ask "Why?" again
4. Repeat until you reach a root cause (typically 5 iterations)
5. Frame the problem around the root cause

**Example:**
- Problem: "We need more subscribers"
- Why? "Because we need more revenue"
- Why? "Because our current revenue is insufficient"
- Why? "Because we haven't monetized effectively"
- Why? "Because we don't have enough engaged readers"
- Root: "How might we create more value for our current readers?"

---

### A3. Success Criteria Definition
**Purpose:** Define what success looks like before generating solutions.

**When to use:** User hasn't specified what winning looks like.

**How to apply:**
1. Ask: "What does success look like in numbers or moments?"
2. Ask: "How will we know we've solved this?"
3. Ask: "What are the non-negotiables?"
4. Use answers to frame the problem with explicit success metrics

**Example:**
- Instead of: "Grow our newsletter"
- Framed: "Add 200 real readers in 60 days, inside 3 hours/week, without increasing costs"

---

### A4. Constraint Mapping
**Purpose:** Make constraints explicit and visible.

**When to use:** Constraints are scattered or implicit.

**How to apply:**
1. List all constraints: time, money, effort, skill, maintenance appetite
2. Categorize as hard (cannot violate) vs. soft (can potentially bend)
3. Use constraints to eliminate entire classes of solutions upfront

---

### A5. Assumption Smash
**Purpose:** Surface and challenge hidden assumptions.

**When to use:** Problem statement keeps wobbling or feels stuck.

**How to apply:**
1. List all assumptions implicit in the current framing
2. For each assumption, ask: "What if this isn't true?"
3. Generate alternative framings based on smashed assumptions

**Example:**
- Assumption: "We need more subscribers to grow revenue"
- Smash: "What if we could increase revenue from current subscribers?"
- New framing: "How might we monetize our existing 400 subscribers more effectively?"

## B. Idea Generation Techniques

### B0. Mechanism Map
**Purpose:** Ensure structural divergence by mapping mechanisms explicitly.

**When to use:** Options are wording-twins of each other.

**How to apply:**
1. For each generated idea, identify its core mechanism
2. If mechanisms repeat, eliminate the weaker variant
3. Force new mechanisms for subsequent ideas

**Mechanism categories:** borrowed audience, paid referral, SEO artifacts, scarcity ritual, direct outreach, content repurposing, partnership, gamification, etc.

---

### B1. Analogy Raid
**Purpose:** Borrow solutions from analogous domains.

**When to use:** Everything is safe and predictable.

**How to apply:**
1. Pick an analogous domain (e.g., "How would a YouTube channel solve this?", "How would a SaaS company solve this?")
2. Generate solutions in that domain
3. Translate solutions back to the original problem

**Example:**
- Problem: Growing newsletter subscribers
- Analogy: "How would a podcast grow listeners?"
- Solutions: Guest appearances, cross-promotion, exclusive content
- Translated: Guest issues, newsletter swaps, subscriber-only content

---

### B2. Inversion
**Purpose:** Find solutions by considering the opposite.

**When to use:** Everything is safe and predictable; need absurd options.

**How to apply:**
1. Invert the goal: "How could we guarantee failure?"
2. Generate ways to achieve the inverted goal
3. Invert those solutions to get original solutions
4. Some inverted solutions may be viable as absurd options

**Example:**
- Goal: Get more subscribers
- Inverted: "How could we lose all subscribers?"
- Methods: Stop publishing, spam people, make content terrible
- Inverted back: Start publishing consistently, respect people's time, improve content
- Absurd option: Publicly unsubscribe inactive readers (inversion of "keep everyone")

---

### B3. Worst Idea First
**Purpose:** Break out of conventional thinking.

**When to use:** Everything is safe and predictable.

**How to apply:**
1. Deliberately generate the worst possible ideas
2. For each worst idea, ask: "What's actually interesting about this?"
3. Refine or use as absurd option

**Example:**
- Worst idea: "Delete all existing subscribers and start over"
- Interesting aspect: Removes dead weight, creates scarcity
- Refined: "Publicly cull to most-active readers quarterly"

---

### B4. Random Stimulus
**Purpose:** Introduce randomness to spark new connections.

**When to use:** Stuck in a rut.

**How to apply:**
1. Pick a random word, object, or concept
2. Force a connection to the problem
3. Generate ideas based on that connection

**Example:**
- Random word: "Ladder"
- Connection: Progress, steps, climbing
- Ideas: Referral ladder (get rewards for bringing others), content ladder (progress through levels), subscription ladder (tiered access)

---

### B5. Constraint Squeeze
**Purpose:** Use constraints as creative catalysts.

**When to use:** Everything is safe and predictable.

**How to apply:**
1. Take an existing constraint
2. Make it more extreme (e.g., "3 hours/week" → "30 minutes/week")
3. Generate solutions that work under the extreme constraint
4. Some solutions may work for the original constraint too

**Example:**
- Constraint: 3 hours/week
- Extreme: 30 minutes/week
- Solutions: Automate everything, template-based, repurpose existing content
- Viable: Automated newsletter swaps, template-based guest issues

---

### B6. Resource Raid
**Purpose:** Leverage existing assets as idea fuel.

**When to use:** User has existing assets (archives, audience, prior attempts).

**How to apply:**
1. List all existing assets: archives, audience segments, prior attempts, skills, tools
2. For each asset, ask: "How could this help solve the problem?"
3. Generate ideas that explicitly use these assets

**Example:**
- Assets: 40 issues, 400 readers, #12 is best issue
- Ideas: "Best of" guides from archives, reader contributions, repurpose #12 as lead magnet

---

### B7. Role Storming
**Purpose:** Generate ideas from different perspectives.

**When to use:** Need diverse perspectives.

**How to apply:**
1. Pick different roles/stakeholders (user, competitor, expert, beginner, etc.)
2. For each role, ask: "What would this person do?"
3. Generate ideas from each perspective

---

### B8. SCAMPER
**Purpose:** Systematically modify existing ideas.

**When to use:** Have some initial ideas but need more.

**How to apply:** For each existing idea, ask:
- **S**ubstitute: What can be swapped out?
- **C**ombine: What can be combined with it?
- **A**dapt: What can be adjusted?
- **M**odify: What can be changed?
- **P**ut to another use: How else can it be used?
- **E**liminate: What can be removed?
- **R**earrange: What can be reorganized?

---

### B9. The Absurd Slot
**Purpose:** Ensure one deliberately ridiculous option per session.

**When to use:** Always (exactly one per session).

**How to apply:**
1. Generate an intentionally absurd idea
2. Apply the same rigor: mechanism, pitch, constraint line
3. Let it compete on merit or die in the kill list

**Example:**
- **Quarterly unlaunch** *(the absurd one)* — *mechanism: scarcity ritual.* Once a quarter, publicly cull to the 100 most-active readers.

---

### B10. Forced Connection
**Purpose:** Create connections between unrelated concepts.

**When to use:** Ideas feel disconnected.

**How to apply:**
1. Pick two unrelated concepts
2. Force a connection between them
3. Generate ideas from that connection

---

### B11. Swap the Actor
**Purpose:** Change who performs the action.

**When to use:** Options are predictable; need new mechanisms.

**How to apply:**
1. For each existing idea, identify the actor (who does it)
2. Swap the actor with someone else (user, third party, automated system, etc.)
3. Generate new ideas with swapped actors

**Example:**
- Original: You write guest issues (actor: you)
- Swapped: Readers write guest issues (actor: readers)
- Swapped: AI generates guest issues (actor: AI)

---

### B12. Change the Medium
**Purpose:** Change the channel or format.

**When to use:** Options are predictable; need new mechanisms.

**How to apply:**
1. For each existing idea, identify the medium (email, web, social, etc.)
2. Change the medium
3. Generate new ideas in the new medium

**Example:**
- Original: Newsletter swap (medium: email)
- Changed: Podcast cross-promotion (medium: audio)
- Changed: Instagram collaboration (medium: social)

## C. Perspective Shifting Techniques

### C1. Beginner's Mind
**Purpose:** Approach the problem as if you're seeing it for the first time.

**When to use:** Over-familiarity is blocking creativity.

**How to apply:**
1. Ask: "What would someone who knows nothing about this industry do?"
2. Generate ideas without industry constraints
3. Filter for feasibility

---

### C2. Competitor Analysis
**Purpose:** Learn from what others are doing.

**When to use:** Need external perspective.

**How to apply:**
1. Identify direct and indirect competitors
2. Analyze their approaches
3. Generate ideas based on their successes and failures
4. Adapt to your context

---

### C3. Future Pace
**Purpose:** Think from the future back to the present.

**When to use:** Long-term planning or big decisions.

**How to apply:**
1. Imagine it's 5 years in the future and the problem is solved
2. Ask: "How did we get here?"
3. Work backwards to identify the steps that led to success
4. Generate ideas based on those steps

---

### C4. The Observer
**Purpose:** Observe the problem from an outsider's perspective.

**When to use:** Too close to the problem.

**How to apply:**
1. Imagine you're an external consultant observing the situation
2. Ask: "What would I recommend to a client in this situation?"
3. Generate ideas based on that perspective

## D. Decision and Convergence Techniques

### D1. Weighted Scoring
**Purpose:** Objectively compare options.

**When to use:** Too many options, no conviction.

**How to apply:**
1. Define criteria important to the decision
2. Weight each criterion by importance
3. Score each option against each criterion
4. Calculate weighted scores
5. Use scores to inform ranking

---

### D2. Pros and Cons Matrix
**Purpose:** Systematically evaluate options.

**When to use:** Need to compare options thoroughly.

**How to apply:**
1. For each option, list pros and cons
2. Categorize pros/cons by importance
3. Identify deal-breakers
4. Use matrix to inform ranking

---

### D3. Weighted Scorecard
**Purpose:** More structured weighted scoring.

**When to use:** Ranking feels vibes-y; need objective comparison.

**How to apply:**
1. Define 4-6 key criteria
2. Assign weights (total = 100%)
3. Score each option 1-10 on each criterion
4. Calculate weighted scores
5. Document rationale for each score

**Example:**
| Criteria | Weight | Option 1 | Option 2 | Option 3 |
|----------|--------|----------|----------|----------|
| Cost | 30% | 8 | 5 | 9 |
| Impact | 40% | 7 | 9 | 6 |
| Speed | 20% | 6 | 8 | 5 |
| Risk | 10% | 8 | 4 | 7 |
| **Total** | **100%** | **7.1** | **7.0** | **6.8** |

---

### D4. Premortem
**Purpose:** Identify potential failure modes before committing.

**When to use:** User says "yes, but..." repeatedly; need to surface concerns.

**How to apply:**
1. Assume the chosen option has failed spectacularly
2. Ask: "What went wrong?"
3. Generate all possible failure modes
4. For each, ask: "How could we prevent this?"
5. Use insights to refine the option or selection

---

### D5. Success Criteria Validation
**Purpose:** Ensure the chosen option meets success criteria.

**When to use:** Before finalizing a decision.

**How to apply:**
1. Take the success criteria from Phase 0
2. For the chosen option, ask: "Does this meet each criterion?"
3. If not, either adjust the option or choose a different one

---

### D6. Stakeholder Impact Analysis
**Purpose:** Consider how options affect different stakeholders.

**When to use:** Decisions affect multiple parties.

**How to apply:**
1. Identify all stakeholders
2. For each option, assess impact on each stakeholder
3. Use insights to inform ranking

---

### D7. Overturn Condition
**Purpose:** Define what would change the ranking.

**When to use:** Always (required for every ranking).

**How to apply:**
1. After ranking, ask: "What evidence or constraint change would flip this ranking?"
2. Define specific, testable conditions
3. Include in the ranking section

**Example:**
- *Overturn: if 2 of the 4 emails get no reply by Friday, #3 wins by default — decision pre-made, no re-litigation.*

---

### D8. Kill/Park Decision Matrix
**Purpose:** Systematically decide what to kill vs. park.

**When to use:** Too many options, need to converge.

**How to apply:**
1. For each option, assess:
   - Is it weak/fundamentally flawed? → Kill
   - Is it good but not for now? → Park
   - Is it strong and ready? → Keep
2. For parked items, define the condition that would bring them back
3. Document both killed and parked items explicitly

## Technique Selection Guide

| Situation | Primary Technique | Secondary Technique |
|-----------|-------------------|---------------------|
| Options are too similar | Mechanism Map (B0) | Inversion (B2) |
| Problem is unclear | HMW Ladder (A1) | Assumption Smash (A5) |
| Need absurd options | Worst Idea First (B3) | Inversion (B2) |
| Too many options | Weighted Scorecard (D3) | Kill/Park Matrix (D8) |
| Ranking unclear | D3 + D7 | Premortem (D4) |
| User keeps objecting | Premortem (D4) | Success Criteria (A3) |
| Constraints are blocking | Constraint Squeeze (B5) | Resource Raid (B6) |
| Need fresh perspective | Analogy Raid (B1) | Role Storming (B7) |
| Stuck in a rut | Random Stimulus (B4) | SCAMPER (B8) |

---

## Notes

- Techniques should be **used by their fruit, not their names** — reference the actual ideas generated, not the technique name
- **1-2 techniques per session** is typically sufficient
- **Remixes count only when the mechanism changes** — don't count cosmetic variations
- The **absurd option** should use the same techniques and rigor as serious options

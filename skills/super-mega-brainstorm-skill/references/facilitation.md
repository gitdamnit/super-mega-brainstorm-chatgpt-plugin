# Facilitation: One Model, One User, or a Room

A group brainstorm fails in predictable ways: the senior person speaks first, the loudest idea becomes the anchor, dissent stays unsaid, and the meeting ends with a list and no owner. This guide supports a single model and user as the default, with optional groups or subagents. It does not create a separate abbreviated workflow: the complete stages and the complete Advanced Smell Test still run.

## Roles

| Role | Job |
|---|---|
| **Decision owner** | Owns the final call and the consequences. Named before the session. |
| **Facilitator** | Runs the process, not the content. Does not advocate while facilitating. |
| **Participants** | Generate, critique, and rank. Bring evidence, not just opinions. |
| **Scribe** | Captures claims, candidates, decisions, and reopen conditions verbatim. |
| **Dissenter** | Argues against the leading option for a fixed period. Rotate the role. |

If the decision owner is also the facilitator, appoint a separate dissent role. Otherwise the room will optimize for agreement.

For a single model and user, the model can facilitate and keep the ledger, but it must not pretend to be an independent panel. Simulated roles are perspective prompts, not independent evidence.

## Before the session

Send a one-page brief:

- The decision question, in one sentence.
- The desired outcome and success evidence.
- Constraints: budget, deadline, must-not-break, authority.
- The baseline or zero option as a comparator.
- The claims already treated as facts, and the claims that are assumptions.
- Pre-work: each participant brings at least one candidate mechanism (if they have one) and one objection. Do not set an idea quota.

Pre-work is not optional. A group that arrives cold will generate variations of the first thing said.

## A 75-minute agenda

| Time | Step | What happens |
|---|---|---|
| 0–5 | Frame | Read the decision question, baseline, and constraints aloud. Confirm the decision owner. |
| 5–15 | Silent brainwriting | Each person writes candidates alone. No discussion. |
| 15–25 | Share and cluster | Round-robin one candidate each; cluster by mechanism, not topic. |
| 25–40 | Deepen | Small groups build the strongest clusters and add assumptions, costs, and constraints. |
| 40–50 | Kill/qualify pass | Hostile read. Name fatal flaws, twins, hard-constraint violations, and unverified assumptions. Do not permanently kill on an untested assumption. |
| 50–60 | Evaluate | Choose criteria before scoring. Silent ordinal scoring, then discuss outliers. |
| 60–70 | Attack and pre-mortem | "It failed in six months. Why?" Top causes, each with a tripwire. |
| 70–75 | Close | Decision owner names the outcome: recommendation, open menu, defer, investigate, reject premise, change problem, one defensible option, or no defensible solution. Name the next test, owner, and review date. |

For a two-hour session, add a second divergence wave after the first kill/qualify pass. Do not add slides.

## Independent collection

- **Brainwriting:** silent writing before speech. The single highest-leverage change to a group brainstorm.
- **Silent sort:** participants cluster independently, then compare names. Disagreement about names reveals different mental models.
- **Dot voting with criteria:** a small number of votes each, one stated criterion. Criteria first, votes second.
- **Assigned dissent:** one person argues the leading option down for five minutes. Rotate the role.
- **1-2-4-All:** individuals → pairs → fours → whole group. Scales ideation without turning into a lecture.

## Preventing anchoring and premature consensus

- Collect ideas before anyone states a favorite.
- Have the decision owner speak last in the first round.
- Ask each participant for one disconfirming observation before the vote.
- Separate generating from evaluating. Do not let a senior person's "this won't work" end the divergence.
- If everyone agrees quickly, assign a red team and ask what evidence would change their mind.
- Record the strongest dissent even if it loses.

## Conflict and tradeoff analysis

When participants disagree, classify the disagreement:

| Type | Resolution |
|---|---|
| **Factual** | Identify the evidence that would settle it; assign a test. |
| **Causal** | Compare mechanisms, confounders, and identification. |
| **Values** | Make the value tradeoff explicit; the decision owner chooses. |
| **Risk tolerance** | State the acceptable downside and who bears it. |
| **Prediction** | Separate the forecast from the preference; test the forecast. |
| **Definition** | Lock terms before continuing. |

Do not average away a values conflict. Do not treat a factual disagreement as a personality conflict.

## Structured synthesis

End with:

- The candidate ledger, including rejected and deferred options with reasons and reopen conditions.
- The criteria and weights, including who chose them.
- The decision status and owner.
- The strongest dissenting view, recorded rather than smoothed over.
- The cheapest discriminating test, the review date, and the tripwire.
- What is explicitly not being done.

## Accountability

- A decision without an owner is a suggestion.
- A rejection without a reopen condition is a permanent loss of information.
- A test without a pass/fail signal is a ritual.
- A meeting without a written ledger is a conversation that will be re-litigated.

## Remote and asynchronous

- Send the brief and pre-work at least 48 hours ahead.
- Collect candidates in a shared document before the call.
- Use silent scoring in the chat; discuss only the outliers.
- Record the decision, ledger, dissent, and owner within 24 hours.
- If the group cannot meet, run a 48-hour async sprint: brief → individual candidates → cluster → vote → decision owner chooses status → pre-mortem in the thread.

## Anti-patterns

| Anti-pattern | Repair |
|---|---|
| The boss speaks first | Silent brainwriting before discussion. |
| The first idea becomes the anchor | Generate in parallel, then share round-robin. |
| Everyone agrees too quickly | Assign dissent; ask what would change their mind. |
| The loudest person wins | Silent scoring, then discuss only outliers. |
| The meeting ends with a list | End with a status, owner, test, and review date. |
| The same people generate everything | Pre-work and small-group rounds; collect written candidates. |
| "We need more research" | Name the cheapest test and the date it reports back. |
| The decision owner is absent | Reschedule. A brainstorm without the owner is a suggestion box. |
| A simulated panel is treated as independent | Label simulated perspectives; seek real sources. |
| A quota forces filler | Stop when the space is provisionally saturated; report zero or one honestly. |
